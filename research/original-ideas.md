# Original Ideas — consolidated hypothesis ledger

Stage A artefact · 2026-10-17

This ledger deduplicates every programme-relevant idea found in the 4 pasted documents and the
18 conversation archives. Line-level evidence is in `research/audit/archives-part1.md` and
`archives-part2.md` (IDs I1–I52 and I-01–I-47 there map onto H-numbers here). Origin labels
follow the mandate strictly:

- **USER IDEA**: Moheet proposed it in a user turn.
- **USER QUESTION**: Moheet only asked, and an assistant answered.
- **ASSISTANT**: generated by an earlier AI (ChatGPT, Gemini or another Claude session). This
  includes AI text pasted into user turns.
- **ESTABLISHED**: demonstrated in credible literature, which is named.
- **INFERENCE**: follows by combining established results.
- **NEW HYPOTHESIS**: proposed here and needing independent verification.

Verdict column: **keep** (feeds a candidate), **merge** (into another entry), **reject**
(wrong, trivial, or dominated by prior art), **flag** (overclaim or unverifiable).

## 8. New hypotheses generated in this programme (require verification)

| ID | Statement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| N-1 | **Circularity principle.** Money is needed to settle intermediate (B2B) obligations only for the **acyclic** part of the obligation graph. The cyclic part (its bounded maximum circulation) is a pure coordination failure that an information institution can remove without money, custody or new credit. | INFERENCE from Kocherlakota (memory replaces money) and Ljungqvist–Sargent §8.12 (netting makes money redundant), made operational by circulation theory. The novel content is the measurement, not the principle. |
| N-2 | **Proposition 1.** After optimal set-off the residual obligations are acyclic. Settling them in topological order needs exactly Σ net debit positions in cash, the same as a central counterparty with novation. Custody-free clearing plus payment sequencing therefore reaches the netting liquidity bound. | Proven (`experiment-001-spec.md` §3); verified numerically in tests |
| N-3 | **Proposition 2 (cold start).** Under random participation p, expected clearable value is at least Σ over cycles C of w_C·\|C\|·p^\|C\|. With typical cycle lengths around 10 (Republika Srpska), random recruitment is hopeless and cluster recruitment is required. | Proven lower bound. **Exp. 001: supported.** Anchor recruitment clears 5.9× random at p = 0.2 and ≈27× at p = 0.05 |
| N-4 | **Proposition 3 (no free money).** Clearing preserves every firm's net position, so it creates no wealth. Its welfare gain comes only from heterogeneous financing costs, avoided default losses and coordination. Large firms that profit from paying late lose float, so individual rationality can fail for them. | Proven identity; IR problem flagged for design |
| N-5 | **Proposition 4 (British Eagle externality).** Clearing a cycle through a firm that later becomes insolvent transfers c·(l−a)/(l(l−c)) per £ of claim from its non-cleared creditors to its cleared creditors. | Proven algebra. **Exp. 001 E4:** ≈0.7p per £ cleared moves to participants under a 5 % default shock; a distress screen cuts it by 50–90 % |
| N-6 | **Aggregation bias.** Clearability estimated on sector-level input-output data overstates firm-level clearability by at least 2×. | **Exp. 001: rejected.** Sector-level 58.8 % vs firm-level 51.7 % = 1.14× |
| N-7 | **Topology predictor.** Clearability at full participation is predicted out of sample by reciprocity, the obligation weight inside the largest strongly connected component, and mean degree (R² ≥ 0.7). | **Exp. 001: rejected** (out-of-sample R² = 0.37). The gap identified by the prior-art audit remains open |
| N-8 | The UK **Commercial Payments Bill** (mandatory statutory interest, 60-day cap, SBC fines) turns large late payers' float into a liability. Set-off then becomes individually rational for them, especially if a set-off discharge counts as payment. | HYPOTHESIS. Needs legal confirmation that set-off counts as payment for payment-practice reporting and statutory interest. |
